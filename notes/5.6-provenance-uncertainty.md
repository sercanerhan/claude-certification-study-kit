title: Provenance & uncertainty in multi-source synthesis
---
## Core idea
A research report is only as trustworthy as its traceability. Every time findings are summarised or merged, the link between a claim and its source can break. Prevent that by making subagents output **structured claim-source mappings** that later stages must keep. Treat disagreement honestly: annotate real conflicts with both sources, and record dates so that change over time isn't mistaken for contradiction.

## What the exam tests
- Attribution gets lost when findings are compressed without keeping claim-source mappings.
- Subagents output structured mappings (source URL, document name, relevant excerpt) that synthesis must **preserve and merge**.
- Conflicting statistics from credible sources: **annotate both, with attribution**. Don't pick one arbitrarily.
- Analysis completes with the conflicts annotated, and the **coordinator** decides how to reconcile them before synthesis.
- Require **publication or collection dates** so temporal differences aren't read as contradictions.
- Reports separate **well-established** from **contested** findings and keep the original characterisations and method context.
- Render content by type: financial data as tables, news as prose, technical findings as structured lists.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** pick the "more authoritative" number and footnote the other → **Instead:** present both with sources and flag the conflict.
- **Trap:** stop the analysis and wait for the coordinator to choose → **Instead:** finish the analysis with the conflict annotated, then let the coordinator reconcile.
- **Trap:** add citations at the end by searching for supporting sources → **Instead:** carry the original source through every step.
- **Trap:** 31% (2019) vs 58% (2024) labelled a contradiction → **Instead:** include the dates. It's a trend.
- **Trap:** convert everything into one uniform format → **Instead:** match the format to the content type.

## Example
```json
{"claim": "Heat-pump installations grew 40% YoY",
 "source": {"url": "https://example.gov/report", "doc": "Energy Report", "page": 12,
            "published": "2024-03"},
 "excerpt": "...installations rose by 40 percent...",
 "conflicts_with": [{"claim": "growth of 12%", "source": "Industry Analysis 2024-05",
                     "note": "different scope: residential only"}]}
```

## Remember
- Each claim travels with its source and excerpt through every stage.
- Conflicts are annotated, not resolved by preference.
- Dates turn false contradictions into trends.
- Keep established and contested findings in separate sections.
- Choose the format by content type.
