## Core idea
In a multi-agent system a **coordinator** (hub) breaks the task down, delegates pieces to specialised subagents (spokes), collects their results and decides what happens next. All communication goes through the hub. That gives you one place to observe the flow, handle errors consistently and control which information each subagent sees. Subagents work in isolated contexts, so the coordinator is also the only component that sees the whole picture.

## What the exam tests
- Hub-and-spoke: the coordinator handles all inter-subagent communication, error handling and routing. Subagents do not talk to each other directly.
- Subagents have **isolated context**. They do not inherit the coordinator's history.
- The coordinator's jobs: decomposition, delegation, aggregation, and choosing **which** subagents to invoke based on the query's complexity, rather than always running the full pipeline.
- **Overly narrow decomposition** is a coordinator failure. Subagents can each do their job perfectly and the report still misses whole areas.
- Partition the scope so agents don't duplicate work, by giving them distinct subtopics or source types.
- **Iterative refinement**: evaluate the synthesis for gaps, re-delegate targeted searches, then re-synthesise until coverage is sufficient.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** the report misses whole sub-areas, so improve the search queries → **Instead:** check the coordinator's decomposition first. If the subtasks were narrow, every downstream agent was "right".
- **Trap:** let subagents call each other directly to save a hop → **Instead:** route through the coordinator for observability and consistent error handling.
- **Trap:** two agents duplicate work, so deduplicate afterwards → **Instead:** partition the scope before delegating.
- **Trap:** route every query through all four subagents → **Instead:** scale the effort to the query. A simple fact lookup may need one agent.
- **Trap:** a shared-state "agent bus" for coordination → **Instead:** keep control flow in the coordinator.

## Example
Query: "impact of AI on creative industries"
- Bad decomposition: digital art / graphic design / photography (all visual arts).
- Good: visual arts / music / writing & publishing / film & video, each assigned to a search subagent with distinct source types.
- After synthesis, the coordinator sees "film: only 2 sources", re-delegates a targeted search, then re-runs synthesis.

## Remember
- The coordinator owns decomposition, routing, aggregation and gap checks.
- When coverage is missing, look at decomposition before blaming execution.
- Partition up front, run in parallel, then refine iteratively.
- Everything flows through the hub, with no peer-to-peer channels.
- Anthropic's research system scales effort explicitly: about 1 agent for simple fact-finding, several for comparisons, many for broad research.
