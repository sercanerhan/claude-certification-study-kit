## Core idea
Before you cut human review of model output, prove that accuracy holds **in every segment**, not just on average. A 97% overall score can hide a document type or field that is wrong much of the time. Use segment-level analysis to decide where autonomy is safe, use field-level confidence **calibrated on labeled data** to route review effort, and keep a stratified sample under review so new error patterns get caught.

## What the exam tests
- Aggregate accuracy can mask poor performance on specific document types or fields.
- **Stratified random sampling** of high-confidence outputs measures ongoing error rates and catches novel error patterns.
- **Field-level confidence scores**, calibrated against a **labeled validation set**, set the review thresholds.
- Validate accuracy **by document type and field** before automating high-confidence extractions.
- Send low-confidence or ambiguous/contradictory-source items to humans, so limited reviewer capacity goes where it matters most.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** 97% overall, so remove review → **Instead:** break accuracy down by segment first.
- **Trap:** trust raw model confidence thresholds → **Instead:** calibrate them against labeled data before using them for routing.
- **Trap:** review only low-confidence items forever → **Instead:** also sample high-confidence items, or you never see confident errors.
- **Trap:** review 100% of output indefinitely "to be safe" → **Instead:** that wastes reviewer capacity. Measure, then sample.
- **Trap:** a random sample across all output → **Instead:** stratify, so small but risky segments are represented.

## Example
| Segment | Volume | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| Invoices: total | 60% | 99.4% |
| Invoices: due date | 60% | 98.1% |
| Handwritten receipts: total | 7% | 81.0% |
| **Overall** | | **97.2%** |

Decision: automate typed invoices above a confidence threshold calibrated on 500 labeled documents. Keep handwritten receipts in human review. Sample 2% of auto-approved items per segment every week.

## Remember
- Averages hide weak segments.
- Calibrate confidence on labeled data before routing on it.
- Stratified sampling of high-confidence output catches errors you'd otherwise miss.
- Route ambiguous and low-confidence items to people first.
